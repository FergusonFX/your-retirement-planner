Target at retirement

Rebalance to target

Uses today's total and the target percentages in the Target chart above. Changes smaller than 1% of the total are left alone. Selling inside Tax Deferred and Roth accounts doesn't trigger taxes, so make moves there when you can. Selling in a Brokerage account can create capital gains tax. You don't have to do it all at once: many people rebalance once a year, or move toward the target as retirement gets closer.

Holding groups

Rename, add or remove holding groups, for example add an International group. Changes show up in every dropdown, pie chart and table. Clear a name to go back to the original (shown in gray).

Each group's kind (Stocks, Bonds or Cash) decides how it's used: Bonds and Cash groups are the safe money counted in the Up-year refill and left out of the sector chart. Removing a group moves its holdings, target % and withdrawal plan % into another group of the same kind (or the first group).

Spending guardrails

Year-by-year table

Amounts are in today's dollars. The amount in (parentheses) is the estimated dollars in that year, using your inflation setting. As the years go by, update your age today and balances: when "Plan for age" is your current age, the two match.

How to pick the column: look at how stocks did over the 12 months before you withdraw. Down = fell more than 10%. Flat = between down 10% and up 10%. Up = rose more than 10%. Check VTI's last 12 months (Google Finance opens on the 1-year view; the green or red % next to the price is the 12-month change). Change any percentage to set your own rules. Each column should add up to 100%.

Before and after

How this works: each year in your window, it converts just enough from Tax Deferred to Roth to reach the top of the bracket you picked, counting income you already have (taxable Social Security, rental or DST, other income, and Tax Deferred withdrawals for spending). Withdrawals follow the order picked under Taxes on the Ages, Timing and Taxes page (Standard order when Flat tax is picked), and each year's withdrawals pay the tax worked out from the brackets. The tax on each conversion is paid from Brokerage (Cash only if Brokerage runs out), so the full amount lands in the Roth. "Money left after all taxes" counts Roth, Brokerage and Cash in full and Tax Deferred after 22% tax. It follows one typical market path (your investment mix's average return after inflation), and uses the brackets from the Tax Brackets box (married filing jointly with a spouse, single without), rising with inflation, plus the state tax % under Taxes. RMDs start at 75. All amounts are in today's dollars. Watch two things: conversions before 65 raise your income for ACA subsidies, and Medicare's IRMAA looks at income from two years earlier. Use it to plan with your accountant.

Market assumptions

Yearly returns before inflation, like the numbers on a statement. The planner subtracts inflation each year, so higher inflation means lower real growth. Average is the simple average of yearly returns; volatility is how much they swing.
